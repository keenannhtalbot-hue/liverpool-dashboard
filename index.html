What makes Liverpool, Liverpool?

Founded in 1892 by John Houlding after a rent dispute with Everton. Within 14 years they'd won two league titles. Then the First World War knocked them flat, and the club spent four decades as a middling second-division side before Bill Shankly arrived in 1959.

Shankly's doctrine — work harder than the other team, pass the ball on the ground, treat the supporters as family — turned the club into a winning machine. Bob Paisley quietly won more European Cups than any manager in history. Joe Fagan made it three in five years. Then, on 15 April 1989, 97 supporters died at Hillsborough, and the city itself became part of the club's identity — the anthem You'll Never Walk Alone sung for a century of mourning as much as for football.

Jürgen Klopp's 2015–24 reign ended a 30-year wait for a league title (2019–20) and added a sixth European Cup in Madrid (2019). Arne Slot delivered title number 20 in 2024–25 — the new English top-flight record shared with Manchester United.

Five things a Liverpool fan won't let you forget

  1. Istanbul 2005. 3–0 down at half-time to AC Milan, won on penalties. Gerrard's header started it.
  2. Anfield 4–0 Barcelona 2019. Trent's quick corner, Origi's brace, semi-final second leg, no Messi in the team for it.
  3. The 30-year wait ending in 2020. First league title in three decades, behind closed doors because of Covid.
  4. Shankly, Paisley, the Boot Room. Three European Cups in five years, built on a kitchen wall full of tactics.
  5. 97. The number on the Kop every matchday. Never forgotten.
